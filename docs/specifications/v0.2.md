## 1. Product objective

v0.1 teaches a profitable, single-input production line. v0.2 must teach **investment and synchronization of production chains with two independent material inputs**. Technology is unlocked by *production or sale milestones plus one-time payments in credits*, not by research points, laboratories, or time-delayed research.

The player should face meaningful alternatives: sell raw coal, process iron into gears, or combine coal and iron ingots to produce higher-value steel. The player must be able to inspect why a production line is waiting or blocked and determine whether expansion is economically justified.

### Release outcomes

1. Extend the existing physical grid with coal mining, gears and steel.
2. Demonstrate a generic recipe engine with typed input buffers and deterministic processing.
3. Introduce three manually purchased technology unlocks with explicit prerequisites.
4. Continue the story after the v0.1 prologue, with missions M5–M7 and a contextual tutorial.
5. Preserve all v0.1 saves and economic invariants.
6. Run repeatable, headless economy scenarios as a mandatory CI gate.

### Explicitly out of scope

Power generation and consumption; research points, laboratories and research timers; splitters, storage buildings, underground belts, multi-tile buildings, map expansion, procedurally generated resources, offline production, random prices, multi-item outputs, multiplayer or cloud saves. Do not implement any of these implicitly as a workaround.

## 2. Player experience and progression

### Starting conditions

- Continue to use the 12 × 12 grid, fixed one-tile buildings and one-second logical ticks.
- A brand-new v0.2 save still begins with **300 Cr** and the Chapter I introduction.
- Existing v0.1 players retain their complete factory, balance and progress.
- The v0.2 story segment begins after completion of v0.1 mission M4. A save partway through M1–M4 finishes those missions first.
- Proposal from the v0.1 retrospective: on **new games**, unlock construction of the **basic iron furnace after M2**; on migrated saves, preserve already-built furnaces and grant access if the player has reached M3 or later. This adjustment must not alter economic simulation coefficients.
- The player spends credits to claim eligible technologies. Having the required milestones alone does not deduct money or unlock the building.

### Spatial challenge

Introduce two fixed coal deposits at **(7,9)** and **(5,9)**. Preserve existing iron deposits at (3,5), (2,2), (2,9), and the sales terminal at (10,5). The default sale terminal continues to accept one item per tick from any valid adjacent direction, using deterministic arbitration if contested.

**Migration rule:** a v0.1 save might contain a building on a newly introduced coal tile. The migration must *not* move, delete or replace that building. The coal deposit remains underneath it and becomes usable only if the tile is deliberately cleared. Rendering and the inspector must explain this situation.

All relevant v0.2 products should be attainable on this map without a splitter. Two independent full chains must be simultaneously placeable, as shown below. The acceptance suite will build and exercise them rather than assume feasibility.

### Example gear line (northern approach)

```text
Iron mine (2,2) E -> belt (3,2) E -> basic furnace (4,2) E
 -> belt (5,2) E -> assembler (6,2) E
 -> belts (7,2),(8,2),(9,2) E -> belt (10,2) S
 -> belts (10,3),(10,4) S -> sales terminal (10,5)
```

### Example steel line (western approach, coal from south)

```text
Iron mine (3,5) E -> belt (4,5) E -> basic furnace (5,5) E
 -> belt (6,5) E -> advanced furnace (7,5) E
 -> belts (8,5),(9,5) E -> sales terminal (10,5)

Coal mine (7,9) N -> belts (7,8),(7,7),(7,6) N
 -> advanced furnace (7,5) [coal enters from south]
```

The complete gear and steel examples do not overlap. The terminal's throughput is sufficient for their nominal combined output; this must be confirmed in simulation.

## 3. Buildings, products, recipes and prices

All prices are in **Cr**. Store all money as integer cents. The values below are design hypotheses that must be confirmed or revised based on headless testing and a short human playtest.

| Building | Build cost | Inputs | Outputs | Cycle / capacity | Cost per completed operation |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---:|
| Iron mine (existing) | 45 | Iron deposit | 1 iron ore | 2 ticks | 0.60 |
| Basic furnace (existing) | 80 | 2 iron ore | 1 iron ingot | 4 ticks | 1.60 |
| Conveyor (existing) | 5 | 1 incoming item | 1 outgoing item | 1 tile/tick | 0.05 per item entering a belt |
| **Coal mine (new)** | **65** | Coal deposit | **1 coal** | **3 ticks** | **0.80** |
| **Mechanical assembler (new)** | **130** | **2 iron ingots** | **1 gear** | **5 ticks** | **2.50** |
| **Advanced furnace (new)** | **180** | **2 iron ingots + 1 coal** | **1 steel** | **8 ticks** | **3.50** |

All mines have a one-item output buffer. Assemblers and furnaces have separate per-item input buffers of capacity **4 units of each accepted input type**, and a one-item output buffer. A production cycle reserves and consumes all required input items atomically when it starts. No charge is applied until output is successfully created. If the output buffer is occupied at completion, the machine waits, preserving its active cycle and reserved inputs until delivery is possible.

**Sales prices:**

| Item | Sale price |
|---|---:|
| Iron ore (existing) | 2.00 |
| Iron ingot (existing) | 7.00 |
| Coal | **3.00** |
| Gear | **21.00** |
| Steel | **26.00** |

All items use the same physical conveyor system; transport cost is based on actual accepted belt entries. When a machine has multiple input types, each input is stored separately; surplus coal must never substitute for missing iron ingots. Input may come from any adjacent side *except the building's output side*. The machine must not overwrite or drop a buffered item when its output is blocked.

**Design simplification:** the new assembler makes gears and the new advanced furnace makes steel in v0.2. Internally, all production must use declarative recipe definitions so later versions can introduce recipe selection without reimplementing the simulation engine.

## 4. Technology unlock system

Technologies are purchased **manually** once prerequisites are satisfied and the player has enough credits. All prerequisites are visible, with live progress indicators. Research fees are capital/progression expenses: they reduce cash **but do not count as operating costs** and must not affect the 60-tick operating profit indicator.

| Technology ID | Unlocks | Requirements | Research payment |
|---|---|---|---:|
| `coal_extraction` | Coal mine | Complete M4 (v0.1 prologue) | **80 Cr** |
| `mechanical_components` | Mechanical assembler | Complete M4 and sell at least 20 iron ingots across the save | **120 Cr** |
| `advanced_smelting` | Advanced furnace | Own both earlier technologies, have sold at least 10 coal and 8 gears | **180 Cr** |

- Requirements based on lifetime sales remain satisfied after demolition, balance changes or save/reload.
- Unlock payment is atomic, applied once, and rejected without charge when already unlocked or funds are insufficient.
- An unlock persists across save/reload and is never revoked.
- For a migrated v0.1 save, previously earned sale totals can satisfy requirements; **no replay of old missions is required**.
- Basic iron furnace gating on *new games* is milestone-based (completion of M2) and has no research fee.

The technology menu has three states: **Locked** (show missing prerequisites), **Available** (action and cost), **Unlocked**. Players can pursue coal and mechanical components in either order, but must earn both before advanced smelting.

## 5. Story, tutorial and missions

### Narrative positioning

The v0.2 story is an **interlude after Chapter I** and must not prematurely start the electronics-oriented Chapter II. After the corporation recognizes Sector 9's restored profitability, an old engineering memo reveals that the foundry supplied components to the sealed Prometheus program. Headquarters authorizes limited industrial research funding, but the operator must finance each technology unlock from production profits.

### Story opening (draft English copy)

> **OPERATIONS DIRECTIVE // FOUNDRY RESTORATION**  
> Your first deliveries have restored confidence in Sector 9. A retired foundry blueprint has surfaced in the maintenance archive. It references coal-fired metallurgy and mechanical assemblies once used by Project Prometheus.
>
> Corporate authorization is conditional. Unlock the required technologies, rebuild the process, and demonstrate that advanced products can be produced profitably.

Show this after M4, not before. It can be skipped/replayed without changing state or mission conditions.

### Missions

| ID | Mission | Completion event | Mechanic taught |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M5 — Below the Surface** | Restore coal output | Sell **10 coal** through the physical terminal | New deposits, coal mine, technology purchase |
| **M6 — Mechanical Precision** | Demonstrate assembly | Sell **8 gears** through the physical terminal | Two iron ingots per gear, assembler buffers |
| **M7 — The Old Foundry** | Establish advanced steel production | Unlock advanced smelting and sell **8 steel** through the terminal | Two distinct inputs, production synchronization and bottlenecks |

Missions are sequential, while technology purchase order is flexible. A mission tracks **actual sale events**, not inventory counts or items merely produced. Lifetime totals can satisfy a previously achieved numeric prerequisite, but mission progression events cannot be double-counted on load. M7 completion leads to continued sandbox play and a short Prometheus archive hook, not to a full Chapter II unlock.

### Tutorial design

- Provide a one-time introduction to the technology panel when M4 is completed.
- Highlight the relevant coal deposit and coal-mine cost at M5.
- Explain the difference between a profitable sale and a capital/research investment at M6.
- At M7, show separate counters for `iron_ingot` and `coal` inside the advanced furnace; highlight a missing input or blocked output.
- Never require per-tick clicking. Existing keyboard controls, help, replay, reset, and pause continue to work.

## 6. Economic validation and pacing

### Baseline definitions

A *reference scenario* has a fixed building layout, explicit production state and logical tick count. Use a **warm-up of at least 600 active ticks**, then a **240-tick measurement window** for mixed-cycle scenarios. Also preserve the v0.1 exact 60-tick regression at **58.50 Cr/min**.

For throughput projections, assume a single iron mine makes 30 ore/minute, a basic furnace makes 15 ingots/minute, and one coal mine can make 20 coal/minute when unblocked. Gears and steel each need 2 ingots, so **iron supply limits either line to about 7.5 final products/minute**, even when the processing machine is individually faster. This is a useful design constraint: buying a faster assembler alone does not increase output.

### Analytical steady-state scenarios — NOT yet engine-validated

| Scenario | Route | Sales/min | Revenue/min | Operating costs/min | Est. profit/min |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| v0.1 ingot reference | 1 ore belt, 4 ingot belts | 15 ingots | 105.00 | 46.50 | **58.50** |
| Coal direct sales | Coal mine, 6 belts | 20 coal | 60.00 | 22.00 | **38.00** |
| Gears | 1 ore belt, 1 ingot belt, 6 gear belts | 7.5 gears | 157.50 | 65.25 | **92.25** |
| Steel | 1 ore belt, 1 ingot belt, 3 coal belts, 2 steel belts | 7.5 steel | 195.00 | 78.375 | **116.625** |

Steel breakdown (per simulated minute, theoretical): mining iron 18.00; smelting 24.00; coal extraction 6.00; advanced furnace 26.25; conveyor costs 4.125; revenue 195.00; operating profit **116.625 Cr/min** (shown as approximately **116.63 Cr/min**). An exact 60-tick display may oscillate because product cycles and costs do not all divide evenly into 60 ticks. Golden tests must account for integer cents and window boundaries, not compare a rounded average to a single arbitrary 60-tick window.

**Capital investments:** the suggested standalone gear layout costs 295 Cr (mine 45 + furnace 80 + assembler 130 + eight belts 40). The suggested standalone steel layout costs 405 Cr (iron mine 45 + furnace 80 + coal mine 65 + advanced furnace 180 + seven belts 35). These are construction costs only; unlock payments of 80 + 120 + 180 = **380 Cr** are additional. Existing infrastructure can be reused, reducing incremental spending.

These projections are **targets to challenge**. They must not be declared validated until the v0.2 simulation has measured them, including run-up, resource starvation, buffering, output competition and realistic transitions between missions.

### Economic quality gates

1. v0.1 reference result remains exactly unchanged at 58.50 Cr/min for its locked fixture.
2. Gear and steel lines are physically buildable, self-sustaining, and more profitable than the reference iron-ingot line once operational.
3. Neither gear nor steel generates credits without mining, processing and physical terminal delivery.
4. Research spending changes cash but not operating-profit statistics.
5. Players can reach M7 without an unrecoverable credit deficit. Ordinary mistakes and 75% demolition refunds should not cause an inescapable failure state.
6. Measure the effect of input starvation, blocked outputs, contested terminal delivery and intermittent supply.
7. Report **payback periods** using *incremental capital cost* / *incremental operating profit per minute*; include unlock fees and reusable buildings, not only raw product prices.
8. Target an experienced-player M4→M7 journey of roughly **20–40 minutes** of active play, subject to playtest rather than assertion.

### Automation requirement

Add reproducible fixtures under `tests/integration/economy/` or a comparably organized suite. Produce human-readable scenario results in CI (throughput, sales, costs, profit, money conservation, payback estimate). Exact expected outputs should be pinned only after successful golden-run validation, with rationale for any intentional balance change. Regression checks must run on each PR and must not require a browser.

## 7. Technical design requirements

### Generic recipe-driven engine

Replace hard-coded `furnace.oreInput` logic with typed inventories and recipe definitions. Suggested interfaces:

```ts
type ItemType = "iron_ore" | "iron_ingot" | "coal" | "gear" | "steel";
type RecipeId = "iron_ingot" | "gear" | "steel";

interface RecipeDefinition {
  id: RecipeId;
  input: Partial<Record<ItemType, number>>;
  output: { item: ItemType; quantity: 1 };
  durationTicks: number;
  operatingCostCents: number;
}

interface ProcessorState {
  recipeId: RecipeId;
  inputBuffers: Partial<Record<ItemType, number>>;
  output: ItemType | null;
  cycleRemainingTicks: number | null;
  reservedInputs: Partial<Record<ItemType, number>>;
}
```

The shape is illustrative; the implementation agent can improve it while retaining explicit buffering and reservations. All v0.1 mechanics must continue to use the new engine without behavioral regressions.

### Transport and production invariants

- One source can propose at most one outgoing item per tick.
- An item can move at most one tile per tick.
- Items of any supported type can travel on ordinary conveyors.
- Incoming items are only accepted when the corresponding typed buffer has free capacity.
- All recipe inputs are reserved atomically when a cycle starts.
- A missing input prevents cycle start and charges nothing.
- Output backpressure prevents item duplication and does not repeatedly charge completion costs.
- Simultaneous arrivals compete deterministically and fairly; arbiters survive save/restore.
- All money and inventory operations are integer-valued and reproducible.
- Product conservation can be verified using a per-item event ledger (mined, consumed, produced, sold, explicitly destroyed), rather than relying on a single ore-equivalent formula when multi-input recipes are introduced.

### Save migration

The game-state schema advances from **v1 to v2**. Do not silently reset saves, and do not rename the existing IndexedDB database without a deliberate data-copy migration. Existing single-slot storage and serialized write queue remain valid.

Migration requirements:

- Preserve tick, balance, layout and entity IDs, directions, conveyor items, active cycles, historical sales, cash statistics, story/tutorial state, and current v0.1 mission.
- Convert the existing furnace `oreInput` count to a typed input buffer and preserve any reserved in-cycle ore when an operation is underway.
- Add research state, new item counters, new deposits and new chapter progress with correct defaults.
- Do not remove structures covering newly introduced coal deposits.
- Migrate only once; a v2 save reload must be idempotent.
- Reject corrupt/mismatched data with a recoverable error and no destructive overwrite.
- Preserve hidden-tab pause and **no offline progression**.

### UI enhancements

- A compact **Technology** panel: nodes, prerequisites, sale progress, payment, status, unlock confirmation.
- A recipe view for each new machine, with separate input counts for every required material and the remaining cycle time.
- Clear locked-construction messaging and contextual links to technology prerequisites.
- New visible coal deposits, building colors/icons and transport-item distinctions. Color is never the only signal.
- No new modal interruptions during ordinary production; story dialogue only at appropriate milestones.

## 9. Implementation boundaries and work packages

This is a specification for a **separate v0.2 implementation chat**, not an instruction to implement from the design conversation.

1. **V02-WP1 — Migration and domain model:** versioned save upgrade, typed inventory/recipe definitions and backwards-compatible v0.1 fixtures.
2. **V02-WP2 — Headless production engine:** coal mine, assembler, advanced furnace; deterministic typed material routing and buffering.
3. **V02-WP3 — Economic acceptance suite:** three additional scenarios, original baseline, conservation, payback and blockage tests.
4. **V02-WP4 — Technology progression:** prerequisites, paid unlock commands, persistence and unlock UI.
5. **V02-WP5 — Narrative/tutorial:** M5–M7, story, hints, inspection and feedback.
6. **V02-WP6 — Integration:** E2E playthrough, performance/saving regressions, human playtest, release checklist and PR review.

Implementation order intentionally keeps the headless engine and migration ahead of visual features.

## 10. Approved decisions and implementation validation gates

The product owner approved this design on **2026-11-08**. The following decisions form the implementation baseline:

1. **Economic constants:** proposed building prices, product sale prices, recipe durations and research fees are approved as initial test targets. Validate them in automated scenarios before considering them balanced; document and review any change rather than silently editing values.
2. **Map constraints:** keep the 12 × 12 map and fixed coordinates. Verify simultaneous construction and operation of the reference gear and steel lines; a map change requires a separate approved design decision.
3. **Basic furnace gating:** on new v0.2 games, unlock the basic furnace after M2. Grandfather existing buildings and v0.1 progress during migration. Preserve the original economics benchmark using a suitable unlocked or migrated fixture.
4. **Technology purchase UX:** show a confirmation dialog for purchases, including the fee and one-time nature of the unlock. Do not auto-spend credits; no undo mechanic in v0.2.
5. **Mission pacing:** target 20–40 minutes for an experienced M4→M7 playthrough; use automated economy tests and human playtesting to tune it, with changes documented.

**Implementation gate:** the specification is approved for a separate v0.2 implementation chat on a feature branch. Headless economic validation and migration tests precede UI work. The 24 acceptance criteria above are release gates. Treat the v0.1 baseline as a non-regression requirement.
