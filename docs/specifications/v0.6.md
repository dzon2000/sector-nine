## Player experience

From M1, switch between 1× and 2× while building or waiting. After M13, the reconstructed interlock picks up a maintenance-channel transmission. A station on the other end has been repeating the same restart request since the shutdown. The player verifies that the circuit line can keep running, ships raw copper for the station’s repairs, then restores circuit service. The independent route hears the raw transmission. The corporate route receives the same request through a redacted service ticket. Both reach the same station and objectives. The final reply, “Sector 9, we can see your line again,” establishes a live counterpart for the next chapter.

## Clock and pacing

Start new sessions at 1×. Available from the tutorial with a visible, keyboard-accessible speed selector alongside pause. Switching during play schedules the next tick at the new interval without granting an immediate tick or flushing queued work. Pause/resume retains the selection. Hidden tabs, pause and offline time accrue zero ticks. No catch-up burst. At 2× schedule one ordinary engine tick every 500 ms. Renderer frames never advance simulation. All displayed production rates and mission windows use simulation time; identify that unit in the UI.

Use a controlled clock to verify 60 ticks in 60 or 30 active seconds and zero ticks during pause or a hidden interval. Replay identical actions at identical tick boundaries at 1×, 2× and mixed speeds; compare ledgers, inventory, power, mission events and save/restore. Current-version saves only. In opt-in reports, record rate changes, active wall time and tick counts independently. Human playtest compares early-game waiting, planning and reading separately. Do not infer intent from speed or promise a 50% reduction in total playtime.

## Chapter IV mechanics

Propose a Chapter IV control on existing splitters. Default remains current alternating behavior. Priority mode tries the selected outlet (forward or clockwise), then the other outlet if blocked. Both blocked holds the item. Rotation carries the priority outlet relative to the structure. Changing mode moves no cargo by itself. Save the mode and selection. Expose both outlet directions in the inspector and board overlay. This gives the player a reversible local adjustment on a crowded board.

| Mission | Player decision | Evidence of success |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M14 — Answer the Call | Inspect the incoming request and protect circuit production while sharing a feed with another line. A prepared layout can qualify immediately after activation. | Newly produced and sold circuits meet a modest floor across a short full window. A visible continuity indicator identifies interruptions. |
| M15 — Restore Service | Send a batch of raw copper to the station, then prioritize the circuit line again. A factory designed to do both can qualify without a routing edit. | After activation, sell three raw copper ore. Starting on the next tick after the third sale, qualify for M14’s circuit continuity goal in one fresh 30-tick window. Grant the station reply exactly once. |

Offer Chapter IV explicitly after M13; free play remains available. Reuse contract sampling, existing machinery and the paid 12×12 map. Never require a splitter click or count edits to advance. Before freezing targets, test both paid M13 route endpoints, including a layout where a local routing change visibly resolves starvation. Proposed feasibility gate per endpoint: demonstrate a solution with at most one local branch reworked, document edits and cost, and keep the terminal and main trunk in place. Treat this as a design test, never as a player rule. If the puzzle demands extensive demolition, revise the objective. Prove an unbalanced layout can fail, recovery works, and simply idling at 2× cannot fix a throughput deficit. Checkpoint fixtures allow focused Chapter IV testing; fresh campaign remains the progression regression.

## Sprite production

Initial generated sheet provides art direction for 12 structures and eight cargo items. Prepare individual transparent, trimmed frames with consistent anchors, padding, nearest-neighbor scaling and a PixiJS atlas. Structures: iron mine, coal mine, copper mine, silicon extractor, furnace, advanced furnace, assembler, circuit fabricator, coal generator, conveyor, splitter, sell terminal. Cargo: iron ore, coal, copper ore, silicon, iron ingot, steel, gear, circuit. Use distinct silhouettes, especially ingot vs I-beam and iron vs copper ore. The generated fork graphic needs correction: actual splitter outlets are forward and clockwise. Draw direction and priority overlays from authoritative game state.

Review a live congested line at the existing 44-pixel tile size and supported scaled viewports before final atlas packing. Confirm cargo identity, blocked goods, machine output, selection, placement preview and all four orientations at both speeds. Keep inspector labels, accessible names and occupied-deposit behavior. A belt item must match simulated occupancy even when blocked.
