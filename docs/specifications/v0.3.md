## 1. Product decision

v0.3 replaces the passive delivery-count requirements of M6 and M7 with sustained performance goals. The release teaches players to increase throughput, manage scarce input capacity, maintain parallel product streams, and inspect bottlenecks. M5 remains coal-extraction onboarding.

**Approved functional scope:** mission redesign, player-facing bottleneck diagnostics, and reproducible playtest/behavioral measurements. The numerical mission goals and legacy-save policy below are approved.

**Explicitly deferred:** storage, offline production, map expansion, new recipes/resources, generators, automatic balance changes, and a new campaign chapter. These were originally considered in `docs/roadmap.md` and must be rescheduled explicitly after v0.3.

## 2. Evidence and problem definition

The v0.2 product-owner playtest recorded:

| Segment | Real elapsed time | Active simulation ticks |
|---|---:|---:|
| M5–M7 | 8:00 | 423 |
| Full campaign | 11:44 | 621 |

Only two iron deposits and one coal deposit were necessary. The only meaningful industrial change was adding an advanced furnace and redirecting coal from direct sales to steel production. The player largely waited for credits; higher delivery quotas would reproduce the problem.

The existing reference fixtures independently validate 7.5 gears/min and 7.5 steel/min at steady state when each uses one iron ore extraction chain. The existing engine already supports directional transfers, typed recipe inputs, finite buffers, backpressure, one-item-per-tick terminal transfers, and deterministic input arbitration.

## 3. Approved mission contract

| ID | Name | Exact completion condition | Intended design lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| M5 | Below the Surface | Sell 10 coal through the physical terminal (unchanged). | Unlock and route coal. |
| M6 | Mechanical Precision | Sell **at least 9 gears in each rolling 60-active-tick window**, satisfying that condition for **120 consecutive active ticks**. | One iron-processing chain is insufficient; increase feed capacity and route multiple ingot inputs. |
| M7 | The Old Foundry | With Advanced Smelting unlocked, simultaneously sell **at least 7 gears** and **at least 7 steel** in rolling 60-active-tick windows, while sustaining **at least 175.00 Cr operating profit per rolling 60 ticks**, for **120 consecutive active ticks**. | Maintain two products, reallocate scarce iron, and preserve profitability. |

### 3.1 Shared measurement semantics

1. All product counts come from successful physical terminal-sale events; produced but unsold items do not count.
2. The mission's first valid 60-tick window contains **60 active ticks after mission activation**. Sales made before that activation never contribute to its rolling product counters.
3. The stable-operation timer increments once per active tick only when **every** condition is satisfied. If any condition fails, the timer resets to zero. Completion requires a 120-tick streak after a valid measurement window exists.
4. Pause, hidden-tab pause, save, and reload do not advance ticks or prematurely satisfy or reset the streak. Streak and rolling mission metrics must be serialized or reconstructed deterministically from persisted events.
5. Operating profit is sale revenue minus successful production and transport costs; it excludes research fees and machine purchases/refunds. Use existing integer-cent accounting and the rolling 60-tick window.
6. Technology purchases remain manual and prerequisites are unchanged. M7 requires the permanent `advanced_smelting` unlock.
7. Mission completions are permanent. Mission failure resets only the active streak, not prior milestones.
8. Stockpiling must not offer an easy passive bypass: require adversarial scenarios including a filled single-chain gear output route. Investigate any further accumulation exploit discovered during implementation; thresholds must not be changed without new product-owner approval.
9. The user interface must show actual rolling values, target values, streak length, and the unmet condition. No hidden success rules.

**Approved numerical rationale:** A single iron mine produces 30 ore/min and one furnace 15 ingots/min. Because each gear consumes two ingots, a one-chain factory reaches only about 7.5 gears/min; a 9-gears/min gate requires increasing effective iron supply. A dual-fed assembler prototype reaches 12 gears/min. The approved 7+7 dual-product target approaches the two-line steady-state 7.5+7.5 rate without depending on a particular processing phase. The approved profit gate is below the measured combined reference of 208.875 Cr/min, leaving practical efficiency margin.

## 4. Reference factory layouts (12×12)

Coordinates use `(x,y)`, top-left `(0,0)`. Each named machine occupies exactly one tile. Deposits and terminal are unchanged. `E` and `N` denote output direction. Coal input enters the advanced furnace from the south.

### F-M6: Dual-fed gear assembly

**Primary iron path:** `(2,2)` Iron Mine E → `(3,2)` Conveyor E → `(4,2)` Furnace E → `(5,2)` Conveyor E → `(6,2)` Assembler E → `(7,2)`, `(8,2)`, `(9,2)` Conveyors E → `(10,2)`, `(10,3)`, `(10,4)` Conveyors S → Terminal `(10,5)`.

**Supplemental iron path:** `(3,5)` Iron Mine E → `(4,5)` Conveyor E → `(5,5)` Furnace E → `(6,5)`, `(6,4)`, `(6,3)` Conveyors N → same Assembler `(6,2)` from south.

- Build cost, if constructed from scratch: **440 Cr**, excluding technology fees.
- Exactly two iron mines, two furnaces, one assembler, and 12 conveyors.
- Both furnace feeds converge on one assembler; competing incoming transfers are resolved by the existing deterministic arbiter.
- Exploratory headless-equivalent simulation: **48 gears sold / 240 ticks = 12 gears/min**, approximately **146.40 Cr operating profit/min**.
- One-chain reference: **30 gears / 240 ticks = 7.5 gears/min**, **92.25 Cr operating profit/min**.
- Coal assets acquired in M5 may remain in place temporarily; when the coal route is disconnected, backpressure prevents new output charges.

### F-M7: Concurrent gear and steel production

**Gear path:** The primary iron path from F-M6 remains unchanged.

**Steel iron path:** `(3,5)` Iron Mine E → `(4,5)` Conveyor E → `(5,5)` Furnace E → `(6,5)` Conveyor E → `(7,5)` Advanced Furnace E → `(8,5)`, `(9,5)` Conveyors E → Terminal `(10,5)`.

**Coal path:** `(7,9)` Coal Mine N → `(7,8)`, `(7,7)`, `(7,6)` Conveyors N → Advanced Furnace `(7,5)` from the south.

- Build cost, if constructed from scratch: **700 Cr**, excluding research fees.
- Exactly two independent iron chains, one coal mine, one assembler, one advanced furnace, and a shared terminal.
- Exploratory headless-equivalent simulation: **30 gears and 30 steel sold / 240 ticks**; **208.875 Cr operating profit/min** on average.
- The terminal has 60-item/min nominal input capacity; this layout generates ~15 terminal sales/min, so the terminal is **not** automatically the bottleneck.

### F-M6 → F-M7 conversion

Retain primary gear line and all existing iron/coal machinery. Remove conveyors `(6,4)` and `(6,3)`, rotate `(6,5)` from N to E, add Advanced Furnace `(7,5)` and conveyors `(8,5)`, `(9,5)`. Reuse coal conveyors `(7,8)`, `(7,7)`, `(7,6)`.

**Player decision:** divert the second iron stream from maximizing gear output to enabling steel, while retaining adequate gear production and economic performance. No map growth or new splitter is required.

## 5. Exploratory simulation evidence — NOT official repository test results

The designer recreated the v0.2 tick phases in an isolated analytical script without changing repository files. Its outputs reproduce the published v0.2 reference profits (ingots 58.50, coal 38.00, gears 92.25, steel 116.625 Cr/min), lending confidence to the modeling. **Independent implementation-time tests using the actual TypeScript engine remain mandatory.**

| Scenario | 240-tick gear sales | 240-tick steel sales | Profit/min | Mission gate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| One iron chain → gears | 30 | 0 | 92.25 Cr | M6 fails |
| Two iron chains → assembler | 48 | 0 | 146.40 Cr | M6 passes |
| One chain → steel | 0 | 30 | 116.625 Cr | M7 fails |
| Dedicated gear + steel chains | 30 | 30 | 208.875 Cr | M7 passes |

The approved-target M6 fixture first satisfies 120 consecutive rolling windows at approximately **tick 182** from a cold complete layout (9 gears/60 ticks). The approved-target M7 fixture first meets the `7 gear + 7 steel + 175 Cr` rolling-window conditions for 120 consecutive active ticks at approximately **tick 192** from a cold complete layout. These figures exclude construction and player decision time. They are exploratory planning values, not repository test evidence.

An exploratory single-chain stockpile fixture filled its disconnected gear output route before reconnecting it to the terminal. Its longest streak with at least 9 rolling-window gear sales was only **55 ticks**, short of the 120-tick mission gate. This is an illustrative anti-exploit test, **not proof** that every possible buffer configuration is safe.

Varying construction start offsets for the separate lines kept the simulated steady-state rolling 60-tick profit above approximately **182.6 Cr** across the sampled offsets. Choosing **175 Cr**, rather than 180 Cr, allows more tolerance for discrete tick phases, alternate layouts, and additional conveyor cost.

### 5.1 Staged campaign and cash-flow check

An isolated simulation followed actual construction/rotation/demolition economics, all three technology payments, and no injected income, beginning with 300 Cr:

| Checkpoint | Simulation tick | Cash |
|---|---:|---:|
| M2 → M3 | 26 | 232.50 Cr |
| M4 | 173 | 292.95 Cr |
| M5 | 210 | 184.40 Cr |
| M6 (new throughput gate) | 538 | 354.45 Cr |
| M7 (new parallel-production gate) | 719 | 646.90 Cr |

**Important capital sequencing finding:** Dismantling all terminal routes before saving enough construction capital can temporarily eliminate income and block the planned upgrade. A tested recovery is to retain productive routes while accumulating the required credits, then perform the conversion. The interrupted attempt is **not proof of an irreversible softlock**: the removed conveyor route could still be rebuilt using available cash.

The 719 active ticks are a simulation result, **not** an observed 12-minute human playtest and **not** a basis for inflating costs or idle timers.

## 6. Player-facing bottleneck diagnostics

Use one authoritative deterministic simulation; do not infer operational status from animation or browser wall-clock time.

### 6.1 Per-machine metrics

- **Production rate:** completed units in the trailing 60 active ticks, separately from terminal sales.
- **Working/processing share:** ticks spent executing a processing cycle (definition must specify tick-phase boundary).
- **Input-starved share:** ticks unable to start a cycle because required input material is unavailable.
- **Output-blocked share:** ticks unable to discharge completed output downstream.
- **Current input buffers:** per-material item counts, already available in v0.2.
- **Constraint hint:** one short, actionable explanation: missing ore/ingots/coal, blocked output, or saturated assembler.

### 6.2 Conveyor/terminal metrics

- **Transport rate:** successful item movements through a conveyor over trailing 60 ticks.
- **Blocked dwell time:** occupied conveyor ticks where the item cannot move because downstream cannot accept it.
- **Terminal intake:** physical sales/min by material and combined transfers/min.

A conveyor containing an item must not automatically count as blocked: distinguish a successful forward transfer from a stalled belt. The single terminal's nominal 60 transfers/min capacity must not be portrayed as a bottleneck without evidence.

### 6.3 Mission HUD

Display a compact progress comparison, such as `Gears 8/9 per min`, `Steel 7/7 per min`, `Profit 187.20/175.00 Cr per min`, and `Stable 42/120 ticks`. Highlight the most relevant unmet condition, with inspectable machine/route guidance rather than a forced modal.

**Persistence:** introduce a backward-compatible, versioned save representation for per-material rolling sale events, mission activation tick/streak, and diagnostic state. The v0.2 cumulative material ledger remains authoritative for lifetime material conservation; a new per-tick window is needed for mission-specific rates.

## 7. Save migration and progression

1. Preserve all v0.1 and v0.2 saves, game entities, typed inventories, processing reservations, transport arbiters, research unlocks, cash, lifetime sales, and existing milestone completions.
2. Saves already at `DONE` stay complete; do not retroactively revoke achievements. A replayable, optional performance challenge for such saves may be considered separately, not silently added as a requirement.
3. Existing saves mid-M6 or mid-M7 must not lose progress or be forced into a more difficult goal. **Approved policy:** mark these saves as **legacy mission rules** and allow the original mission to finish; new campaigns use v0.3 objectives. Mission rule version must persist.
4. M5 behavior remains unchanged. A v0.2 save in M5 can transition into the v0.3 missions after M5 completes if the migration explicitly sets the new rule version and communicates that choice.
5. Legacy and new mission evaluation must not double-count terminal sales or charge technologies twice.
6. Saving/loading during a mission streak must result in the same subsequent mission outcome as uninterrupted simulation.
7. Real-world time elapsed between saves must never advance gameplay; offline progression remains out of scope.

## 8. Playtest measurement protocol

Capture **simulation time**, **wall-clock session time**, **actively paused time**, **time spent waiting for sufficient credits**, **time used making construction/routing decisions**, **building placements/rotations/demolitions**, **used deposits**, **machine input-starved/output-blocked tick counts**, and **milestone transitions** separately.

A small optional local playtest log is acceptable; do not store identifying personal data or send telemetry to a server. The game simulation itself must remain free of browser time APIs; wall-clock measurement belongs in a UI/test harness layer.

**Human acceptance hypotheses:** Players should demonstrably inspect a bottleneck and perform purposeful capacity expansion in M6, then reroute supply to maintain parallel production in M7. Count decisions and layout changes; elapsed time alone is not a success KPI. Preserve the existing 20–40 minute pacing aspiration as a playtest question, not a hard gate.

## 10. Approved acceptance criteria

- **V03-AC-01:** M5 remains a physical terminal sale of 10 coal.
- **V03-AC-02:** M6 measures at least 9 gear terminal sales in every rolling 60 active ticks for a 120-tick streak after the initial mission-scoped window.
- **V03-AC-03:** M7 measures at least 7 gear sales, 7 steel sales, and 175 Cr operating profit in every rolling 60 active ticks for a 120-tick streak; Advanced Smelting must be unlocked.
- **V03-AC-04:** Single-chain gear layout fails M6, while the approved dual-fed assembler layout passes M6.
- **V03-AC-05:** Single-product lines fail M7; the parallel gear-and-steel layout passes M7.
- **V03-AC-06:** M6 and M7 are not trivially bypassed with prefilled output belts or reserved recipe inputs; representative stockpile fixtures are tested.
- **V03-AC-07:** An automated new-game campaign reaches the new DONE state from 300 Cr, pays all research fees, and recovers from representative mistakes without credit injection.
- **V03-AC-08:** Throughput counters derive from actual terminal-sale events by item; production counters remain distinct.
- **V03-AC-09:** Profit thresholds use integer-cent accounting over 60 active ticks and never include capital or research fees.
- **V03-AC-10:** Pausing, hidden-tab pause, restoring, and replaying a deterministic tick sequence preserve exact streak and window outcomes.
- **V03-AC-11:** Bottleneck diagnostics identify input starvation, output blocking, and occupied but stalled conveyors using actual engine events.
- **V03-AC-12:** Diagnostics and rolling windows show actionable, comprehensible status without requiring developer tools.
- **V03-AC-13:** Existing v0.1/v0.2 saves remain valid; no achieved milestone or unlock is revoked.
- **V03-AC-14:** In-progress v0.2 M6/M7 saves follow explicitly approved legacy migration behavior.
- **V03-AC-15:** The exact v0.1 58.50 Cr reference and v0.2 coal, gear, and steel reference outcomes remain unchanged.
- **V03-AC-16:** Material conservation, physical movement, and deterministic conflict resolution remain valid with parallel lines and repeated save/restore.
- **V03-AC-17:** The existing 12×12 geography and fixed deposits/terminal remain unchanged.
- **V03-AC-18:** A documented human playtest demonstrates deliberate optimization/reconfiguration, not only increased elapsed time.
- **V03-AC-19:** Playtest reports separate wall-clock duration, active ticks, pause/wait time, decisions, reconfigurations, deposits, and blocked-machine time.
- **V03-AC-20:** Existing v0.2 economic values are never silently retuned; later changes require separate approval and ADR update.

## 11. Approved product decisions

1. **Mission thresholds (approved):** M6 `>=9 gears in each rolling 60 active ticks for 120 consecutive active ticks`; M7 `>=7 gears, >=7 steel and >=175.00 Cr operating profit in each rolling 60 active ticks for 120 consecutive active ticks`. The measurement window must be entirely post-activation.
2. **Save policy (approved):** preserve original cumulative-delivery rules for existing in-progress v0.2 M6/M7 saves; new campaigns use v0.3 objectives. A migrated M5 save adopts v0.3 rules on M6 activation. Existing DONE stays DONE.
3. **Diagnostics scope (approved):** per-machine rolling status, per-conveyor blocked dwell, per-material terminal sales and mission progress; no extra storage/splitter building.
4. **Post-DONE compatibility (approved):** retain completion; optional replayable challenge deferred.

**Implementation boundary:** This specification authorizes a future implementation workflow but does not assert that the candidate scenarios have been verified in the repository. No source-code changes were made in the design conversation.
