## Occupancy, recovery routing and deposits

Candidate A: copper `(8,2)`; silicon `(8,9)`. The real-engine v0.3 DONE fixture **occupies (8,2) with a gear conveyor**; (8,9) is empty. No automatic overwrite, displacement, cleanup or new map is used.

The recovery test explicitly demolishes the gear conveyor at (8,2), receiving its standard 75% refund, and places the copper mine on that tile. The old steel factory remains physically connected and continues selling during M8 and M9. Upper gear throughput ceases because the conveyor is replaced — a real compromise, not a loss of old inventory through migration. M8 sends copper over the existing former gear-terminal route and silicon over an east/south perimeter path. M9 then changes the copper and silicon orientations/routing to feed a new circuit fabricator at (10,3), with output at (10,4) into the original terminal. A second coal mine at (5,9) fuels the new generator at (5,6); the pre-existing coal route continues to power the advanced steel furnace *by supplying material coal*, not electricity. No old fixed terminal/deposit moves.

The result establishes **one constructible and financially recoverable M8/M9 route** from an engine-produced v0.3 DONE fixture. It does **not** certify all potential authentic owner layouts nor M10. Candidate B copper (5,2)/silicon (9,9) is unverified and the copper position (5,2) is also occupied by the reference gear route.

## M10 — actual-engine negative validation

**Current candidate gate:** 11 circuit sales + 5 steel sales + 57/60 demand-covered ticks in every trailing 60-active-tick window, for 60 consecutive qualifying evaluated ticks. M8/M9 remain research/progression **proxies** until WP2 implements their actual mission state; M10 was evaluated in a WP3 test against real engine terminal events and power demand, with no product-rule implementation.

The recovered operating M9 + steel layout was advanced by 240 active ticks after tick 949. Measurements:

| M10 probe metric | Actual |
|---|---:|
| Circuits sold over 240 ticks | **40** |
| Maximum circuits in any trailing 60 ticks | **11** (transient overlap / phase effects) |
| Maximum steel sales in a trailing 60 ticks | **8** |
| Maximum fully served demand ticks per trailing 60 | **60** |
| Windows meeting all three candidate thresholds | **7** (nonconsecutive) |
| Longest consecutive qualifying streak | **1 tick** |
| Required streak | **60 ticks** |

**Interpretation:** Strong positive steel and power coverage coexist with insufficient sustained circuit throughput. A transient 11-circuit window **does not** establish sustainable production, and 7 isolated qualifying windows do not come close to a 60-window streak.

**Independent supply/topology bound:** With a single copper deposit and a one-ore-every-three-ticks extractor, steady-state output is at most **20 copper ore per 60 active ticks**. Eleven circuits consume **22 copper ore** in the same period. A single fabricator with six powered processing ticks per circuit has a theoretical long-term upper bound of **10 circuits per 60 ticks**. Only one silicon deposit and fixed-direction conveyors without splitters make it impossible to feed two independent continuously running fabricators from the current single silicon source using static routes. The candidate is therefore incompatible with the desired steady-state M10 expansion. Temporarily staged inventory can still create occasional 11-sale windows (as the real probe showed), making stockpile-resistance testing essential after any redesign.

**This is a mandatory design stop.** It must not be treated as a passing WP3 outcome, retuned silently, or replaced by a test that only checks one 60-tick window.

## Decisions needed before WP3 can close

**Recommended design investigation:** preserve intended two-fabricator sustained throughput, but authorize exploration of **deterministic branching transport** (for copper, silicon and generator fuel) and an adequate copper supply change (for example one copper ore per two active ticks). Such a splitter is explicitly outside the original v0.4 contract, so this requires product-owner authorization and fresh WP1 headless contracts before it can be accepted. WP3 must then construct and verify the exact expanded 12×12 layout and save/credit recovery; merely raising the copper rate does not fix the single-fabricator limitation.

**Lower-scope alternative:** reduce/replace M10 goals to reflect one physically supplied circuit fabricator, maintaining simultaneous steel and meaningful nonzero power coverage. This sacrifices the approved requirement to force deliberate factory/electrical capacity expansion and therefore also requires a design decision, not a silent threshold retune.

**Other alternative:** add independently routed second copper/silicon sites and enough independent generator fuel supply on the existing map. This avoids a splitter mechanic but adds deposits and crowding; feasibility and migration safety remain unproven.

No candidate numerical/coordinate final set can honestly be proposed as **validated** until a feasible M10 physical fixture, anti-stockpile adversaries and 60-window boundary tests pass. If a redesign is authorized, repeat WP3 before proceeding to WP2.
